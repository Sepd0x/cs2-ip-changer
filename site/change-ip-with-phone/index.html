Mobile data IP

Change your IP with your phone's mobile data

Turning mobile data off and on usually gets you a new IP from your carrier. CS2 IP Changer does that from the PC over ADB, while your phone shares its connection over USB, and checks that the IP really changed.

How it works

  1. USB tethering (or the phone's hotspot) shares the phone's mobile data with the PC.
  2. Only the game moves. CS2, Steam and your servers go through the phone; the browser, Discord and downloads stay on your home connection and don't eat mobile data. You can send the whole PC through the phone instead.
  3. Change IP turns mobile data (or airplane mode) off and on over ADB until the carrier hands you a new IP, and keeps a list of old IPs so you can see it changed.
  4. New range check. It tells you if the new IP is in a new /16 range, because some admins ban whole ranges, and can keep trying until it is.

What you need

  • Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) and admin rights (routes and firewall rules are system settings).
  • An Android phone with mobile data and a USB cable. USB debugging is needed only to change IP from the PC.
  • Phone setup checks the cable, the USB driver, USB debugging, tethering and the phone's Wi-Fi, and does each step it can.

Not a VPN

Servers see an ordinary mobile connection from your carrier, like any player on a phone hotspot, not a datacenter IP. That's why it also works on servers that block VPNs. A Windows Firewall kill switch keeps CS2 off your home connection if the cable comes out.

FAQ

My carrier uses CGNAT. Is that a problem?

No. You share a public IP with other customers and servers see that shared one. Change IP still works.

Will my ping be worse?

Usually, yes. Mobile data adds latency and jitter. The app measures ping, jitter and loss every 30 seconds and tells you when the connection is weak.

Does it work with a 4G or 5G USB dongle?

It works as the connection: if the app doesn't pick it on its own, choose it under Connection > How the PC connects to the phone. Change IP needs an Android phone with ADB, though.
