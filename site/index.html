CS2 IP ban bypass and home IP protection

Change your CS2 IP with your phone's mobile data

CS2 IP Changer sends CS2 and Steam through your Android phone, so community servers see your carrier's IP and not your home IP. Got IP banned? One click gets a new IP, and the app joins the server again.

Who it's for

Anyone who plays CS2 on community servers and wants their home connection kept out of it.

  • You got IP banned on a community server and want back in.
  • You'd rather keep your home IP out of server logs and ban lists.
  • You switch between alt accounts a lot.
  • You stream and want your home IP and Steam logins off screen.
  • You want the same binds, crosshair and settings on every account.
  • You want matchmaking only in the regions with the lowest ping.

How it works

It isn't a VPN. The server sees an ordinary mobile connection from your carrier, so it also works on community servers that block VPNs and datacenter IPs.

Plug in the phone. USB tethering (or its hotspot) shares the phone's mobile data with the PC. Phone setup checks every step for you.
Only the game moves. CS2, Steam and your servers go through the phone. The browser, Discord and downloads stay at home.
Nothing leaks. If the cable comes out, CS2 loses the connection instead of falling back to your home IP.

Both change the IP a server sees. Only one looks like a normal player.

What it does

Everything you need to play on community servers from a mobile IP, in one window.

Change IP

Turns mobile data off and on over ADB until the carrier hands you a new IP. It can keep trying until the new IP is in a different range.

Change IP and rejoin

When a server kicks or bans you, the app plays a sound and offers a new IP and the last server again. Ctrl+Shift+F10 does it from inside the game.

Kill switch

A Windows Firewall rule keeps your servers and CS2's game traffic on the phone connection only. No phone, no connection.

Steam account switcher

Switches between the accounts saved on your PC without typing passwords, shows VAC, game and trade bans, and skips accounts on cooldown or banned on that server.

Regions

Pick where CS2 matchmaking can put you, with each region's ping through the phone. One Firewall rule for cs2.exe only blocks the regions you turn off.

CS2 config manager

Copies binds, crosshair, sensitivity and video between your accounts: from a main account, from the last one, or the latest change. Never while CS2 is open, always with a backup.

Profiles

Give your accounts a new name, picture, bio and privacy from a template, or make them all look like one account. You see the preview first, and Restore original puts each profile back.

Server list

Paste IP:port or steam://connect links and see name, map, players and ping through the phone. On a full server it can wait for a slot.

Phone setup

Checks the cable, the USB driver, USB debugging, tethering and the phone's Wi-Fi, does each step it can, and tells you the one thing left.

Connection quality

Measures ping, jitter and loss through the phone every 30 seconds and tells you when the connection is weak.

Live checks

Firewall, routes, the IP servers see next to your home IP, and whether Steam uses the same connection as the game. There's a leak test too.

Stream friendly

Your home IP stays masked in the app, and Hide usernames keeps usernames off screen, also in the Log.

Built to be checked

It runs as admin, so you should know exactly what it does to your PC.

Hands off the game

It never touches the game process, its memory or its program files. It changes Windows routes, firewall rules and which Steam account logs in.

Public source, attested builds

The source is on GitHub, and releases are built by GitHub Actions with a build attestation you can check with gh attestation verify.

Signed updates

The app only installs an update whose SHA-256 is signed with the project's key, which isn't on GitHub. You can turn updates off.

No account, no passwords kept

Switching uses the logins Steam already saved. Anonymous usage stats in the beta never include IPs, servers, accounts or names.

Setup

You need Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), admin rights and an Android phone with mobile data.

  1. Download CS2IPChanger.exe from Releases and put it in a folder only admins can change, like C:\Program Files\CS2 IP Changer.
  2. Plug the phone in by USB, turn on USB tethering and turn off the phone's Wi-Fi. Not sure what's missing? Press Phone setup under Phone.
  3. Run the app (it needs admin) and paste the servers you play on into Servers.
  4. Open Connection. With CS2 and Steam running, every check should be green and the IP servers see must not be your home IP.

To change IP from the PC, also turn on USB debugging on the phone. The app can download Google's ADB for you (about 7 MB). The exe isn't code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you the first time: More info > Run anyway. If something breaks your connection, Settings > Turn off protection undoes it.

FAQ

More answers in the README.

Is it a VPN? Does it work on servers that block VPNs?

No, it isn't a VPN, and yes, it works there. A VPN sends your traffic through a datacenter, and many community servers block datacenter and VPN IP ranges. This app sends CS2 and Steam through your own phone's mobile data, so the server sees an ordinary mobile connection from your carrier. There's no VPN server in between and no subscription.

I'm banned on a server. Will this get me back in?

If it's an IP ban, change IP and join again. A ban on your Steam account follows the account, not the IP, so a new IP won't lift it, and your other accounts aren't affected by it. The app marks that account as banned on that server and its network, and Switch won't pick it there.

Some admins ban a whole IP range. Does that matter?

After each change the app tells you whether the new IP is in a new /16 or in the same one as before. If you want it to keep going until the range changes, turn on Settings > Keep changing until the IP is in a new range.

Does the account switcher store my Steam password?

No. It uses the logins Steam already saved on your PC (the accounts where you ticked Remember me). Switching never asks for your password or your Steam Guard codes. The only place you type a password is Sign in on the Profiles page, and it's used once and never saved.

Can this get me VAC banned?

It never touches the game process, its memory or its program files. It changes Windows routing and firewall rules and which Steam account logs in. If you use them, the Configs page copies CS2's config files between your accounts and the Profiles page changes your Steam profiles. It also won't hide a cheat from VAC or any other anti-cheat, so don't use it for that.

Will my ping be worse?

Usually, yes. Mobile data adds latency and jitter, so expect a higher ping than at home. A good 4G or 5G signal helps a lot: put the phone near a window and keep it charging. The ping in Servers is measured through the phone, so you see it before you join, and Connection quality tells you when the connection itself is weak.

My carrier uses CGNAT. Is that a problem?

No. You share a public IP with other customers and servers see that shared one. Change IP still works, and when the carrier gives you the same IP back, the app keeps trying.

Can I use the phone's Wi-Fi hotspot instead of a cable?

Yes. Connect the PC to the hotspot and pick it under Connection > How the PC connects to the phone. The app remembers the network name, and if Windows jumps back to your home router it blocks everything. Change IP works on the hotspot too if the phone is also plugged in by USB with USB debugging on.

Does forcing a region get me banned?

The Regions page doesn't touch the game, its files or its memory. It adds one outbound Windows Firewall rule that stops cs2.exe from reaching Valve's relays in the regions you turned off, so matchmaking plays elsewhere, the same as a bad connection to those relays would. Still, we can't speak for Valve and can't promise anything about how they treat it. Allow all and --restore remove the rule; Turn off protection and No phone mode leave it on.

Why does it need admin?

Routes and firewall rules are system settings, and Windows only lets admins change them. That's also why the exe should live in a folder only admins can change.

Does it send anything about me?

While the app is in beta it sends anonymous usage stats: a random install ID, versions, your country, the mode, minutes open and how often each button is used. Never IPs, servers, accounts, names or anything you typed, and the server keeps no IP. The full list is in the README.
